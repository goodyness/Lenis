Web3 Financial Infrastructure SaaS — Full Project Plan
1. The core idea

Build a non-custodial Web3 financial infrastructure platform that allows businesses and developers to:

Accept crypto payments through an API, checkout and payment links.
Use your infrastructure/API directly to build crypto payment functionality into their own applications.
Create crypto escrow transactions where funds are locked in smart contracts and released according to predefined conditions.

The key principle is:

Your platform facilitates and verifies transactions without taking custody of users' funds.

The merchant's crypto goes to their wallet.

Escrow funds go into a smart contract.

Your company doesn't maintain a giant wallet containing everyone's money.

2. The three concepts

There's an important relationship between them.

Product 1 — Payment Gateway

The user-facing product.

"I want my customers to pay me in crypto."

They get:

The developer infrastructure powering Product 1.

"I want to integrate crypto payments into my own application."

So #1 and #2 aren't really separate businesses.

Product 3 — Crypto Escrow

A separate financial primitive built on top of the same infrastructure.

  │ dispute

Escrow
  │
  ▼
Dispute resolution
  │
  ├── Buyer wins → refund

Then develop the dispute system after the basic escrow mechanism works.

22. Smart contracts

I'd keep the contract architecture as simple as possible initially.

Something conceptually like:

createEscrow()
fundEscrow()
release()
refund()
cancel()

Later:

createMilestone()
approveMilestone()
releaseMilestone()
openDispute()
resolveDispute()

Use audited/reviewed patterns and get a proper security review before real-money deployment.

A smart-contract bug can permanently lose funds.

25. Reconciliation

Don't blindly trust one event.

You want your system to periodically reconcile:

Database says:
Payment = $100

Blockchain says:
Transaction = $100

Match them.

If something doesn't match:

RECONCILIATION ALERT

This is very similar conceptually to what you've already dealt with in payment systems, except now the blockchain provides an independently verifiable ledger.

The biggest thing I'd change from your original idea is this:

Don't think "I'm building a crypto payment website."

"I'm building a non-custodial financial infrastructure layer that happens to start with crypto payments."
